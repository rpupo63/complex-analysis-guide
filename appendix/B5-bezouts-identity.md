<div class="env"><span class="hd">Theorem (cited).</span> If the integers $a,b\ge1$ are coprime (their only common divisor is $1$), there are integers $x,y$ (one of them usually negative) with $xa+yb=1$.</div>
<p>The Euclidean algorithm finds them: for $a=3$, $b=5$, $2\cdot3-1\cdot5=1$. @lem:the-simple-poles uses it to show that $1$ is the only common root of $z^a=1$ and $z^b=1$.</p>

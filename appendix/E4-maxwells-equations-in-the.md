title: Maxwell's equations in the plane

<p>In three dimensions, the static part of Maxwell's equations reads $\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=\rho/\varepsilon_0$ (Gauss's law) and $\nabla\times\mathbf E=-\partial_t\mathbf B$ (Faraday's law), which is $0$ when nothing changes in time. For long straight wires perpendicular to the plane, the field does not depend on the coordinate along the wires and has no component in that direction, so it is a planar field $(u,v)$. The 3-D curl then has only one nonzero component, the scalar $\operatorname{curl}$ of [A.5](apx:A5). Choosing units with $\varepsilon_0=1$ gives @hyp:the-laws-of-electrostatics. In these units a unit line charge produces the field $\class{ntn-E0}{E_0}$ of @def:the-field-of-a-unit-charge, of strength $\frac{1}{2\pi r}$: its flux through any circle around it is $1$.</p>

<p>Lecture 4 writes the point $(x,y)$ as $z=x+iy$. Nothing about complex multiplication is used except the following translations.</p>
<ul>
<li>$|z-z_0|=\sqrt{(x-x_0)^2+(y-y_0)^2}$ is the distance between two points.</li>
<li>$e^{i\theta}=\cos\theta+i\sin\theta$ is the unit vector $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$ at angle $\theta$.</li>
<li>$z_0+te^{i\theta}=(x_0+t\cos\theta,\ y_0+t\sin\theta)$ traces the circle of radius $t$ about $z_0$ as $\theta$ runs over $[0,2\pi]$.</li>
<li>$\nabla h\cdot e^{i\theta}$ is the dot product of the gradient with that unit vector: the rate of change of $h$ in direction $\theta$.</li>
<li>$\zeta$ ("zeta") and $\xi$ ("xi") are further names for points, used as integration variables.</li>
</ul>

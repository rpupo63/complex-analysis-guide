title: The complex logarithm and its branches

<p>Write $z\ne0$ in polar form $z=re^{i\theta}$. A logarithm should satisfy $e^{\log z}=z$, which gives $\log z=\log r+i\theta$, but the angle $\theta$ is only determined up to adding $2\pi k$. A <em>branch</em> of $\log$ on a region is a choice of $\theta$ that varies continuously there. On any disk not containing $0$ (more generally, on $\mathbb C$ cut along a ray from $0$) a branch exists, and it is holomorphic with derivative $1/z$. Going once around the origin increases $\theta$ by $2\pi$, so no branch exists on all of $\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}$ (@prop:no-global-primitive-on-mathbb-c). The function $\log|z|=\log r$, the real part, is single-valued and harmonic on $\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}$: it is the logarithmic potential of [E.6](apx:E6).</p>

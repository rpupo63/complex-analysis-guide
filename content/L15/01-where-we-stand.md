<p>The Riemann mapping theorem (@thm:riemann-mapping-theorem-general, Lecture 14) gives a conformal map of every nonempty simply connected open set $\Omega\ne\mathbb C$ onto $D(0,1)$. Composed with the inverse of the Cayley map (@prop:cayley-map), it gives one onto $\Omega$ from the upper half-plane $\mathbb H$. The explicit maps so far are affine maps, powers, $e^z$, $\log z$, the Cayley map $C$ and the automorphisms $\varphi_a$ (@def:automorphism-varphi-a). They are enough for sectors, strips and half-disks (@sec:n11).</p>
<p>This lecture covers:</p>
<ul>
<li>the method of images and the reflection principle, which extend a function across a segment;</li>
<li>the Schwarz–Christoffel formula, an explicit conformal map of $\mathbb H$ onto a polygon;</li>
<li>examples: a half-strip, triangles, rectangles, and a doubly periodic function;</li>
<li>electrostatics: the field near a corner, and at the edge of a capacitor.</li>
</ul>

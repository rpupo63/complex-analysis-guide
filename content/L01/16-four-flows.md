<p>Streamlines $\{\operatorname{Im}f=c\}$, and level sets $\{\operatorname{Re}f=c\}$ of the velocity potential, for four complex potentials:</p>
<figure><div class="fpanels"><div class="fpanel">{{fig:x16-1}}<div class="flab">$z^2$</div></div><div class="fpanel">{{fig:x16-2}}<div class="flab">$\log z$</div></div><div class="fpanel">{{fig:x16-3}}<div class="flab">$-i\log z$</div></div><div class="fpanel">{{fig:x16-4}}<div class="flab">$z+1/z$</div></div></div><figcaption>Streamlines $\{\operatorname{Im}f=c\}$ (solid) and level sets $\{\operatorname{Re}f=c\}$ (dashed) on $[-2,2]^2$.</figcaption></figure>
<ul>
<li><b>$z^2$:</b> flow into a corner. Stagnation point at $0$: streamlines spread apart, speed $0$. (@eg:f-z-z, doubled.)</li>
<li><b>A branch of $\log z$:</b> source. <b>A branch of $-i\log z$:</b> vortex, since $\operatorname{Im}(-i\log z)=\operatorname{Im}(-i\log r+\theta)=-\log r$, whose level sets are circles. Neither extends holomorphically across $0$: the flux out of (respectively the circulation around) a circle about $0$ is $2\pi\ne0$, impossible for an incompressible, irrotational flow on a disk containing $0$ (@prop:1-z-has-a-genuine-singularity).</li>
<li><b>$z+1/z$:</b> uniform flow past the unit disk; the circle $|z|=1$ is itself a streamline ($\psi=0$).</li>
</ul>
<p>The level sets of $\operatorname{Re}f$ and $\operatorname{Im}f$ always cross at right angles where $f'\ne0$, because $\nabla\psi$ is $\nabla\varphi$ rotated by $90°$ (@sec:x11). Lecture 2 reinterprets the same pictures as electric fields.</p>

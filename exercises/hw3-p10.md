Let $f$ be holomorphic on a neighborhood of $\{|z|\le 1\}$. Suppose that $|f|\le A$ on the arc $\{e^{i\theta}:0\le\theta\lt 2\pi/3\}$ and $|f|\le B$ on the rest of the unit circle. Prove that
\[ |f(0)|\le\frac{A+2B}3\qquad\text{and}\qquad |f(0)|\le A^{1/3}B^{2/3}. \]
<details class="sol"><summary>Hint</summary>
For the first bound, write $f(0)$ as the average of $f$ over the unit circle (@thm:cauchys-integral-formula). For the second, consider $h(z)=f(z)\,f(\omega z)\,f(\omega^2 z)$ with $\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$. Exactly one of $z$, $\omega z$, $\omega^2 z$ lies in the arc.
</details>
<details class="sol"><summary>Solution</summary>
<ol>
<li><em>The average.</em> By @thm:cauchys-integral-formula at the centre, $f(0)=\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{|z|=1}f(z)/z\,dz$. Parametrizing $z=e^{i\theta}$ gives $dz/z=i\,d\theta$, so $f(0)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}f(e^{i\theta})\,d\theta$. Therefore
\[ |f(0)|\le\frac1{2\pi}\Big(A\cdot\frac{2\pi}3+B\cdot\frac{4\pi}3\Big)=\frac{A+2B}3. \]</li>
<li><em>One point in the arc.</em> Let $\omega:=e^{2\pi i/3}$, and let $A_0=\{e^{i\theta}:0\le\theta\lt 2\pi/3\}$. The three half-open arcs $A_0$, $\omega A_0$ and $\omega^2 A_0$ are disjoint and cover the circle. For any $|z|=1$, the points $z$, $\omega z$ and $\omega^2 z$ lie one in each arc. So exactly one of them lies in $A_0$.</li>
<li><em>The product.</em> $h(z):=f(z)f(\omega z)f(\omega^2 z)$ is holomorphic near $\{|z|\le1\}$, because $f$ is and $|\omega|=1$. On the circle, step 2 gives $|h(z)|\le AB^2$. Also $h(0)=f(0)^3$. The same average as in step 1 gives $|h(0)|\le\max_{|z|=1}|h|\le AB^2$. So $|f(0)|^3\le AB^2$, and $|f(0)|\le A^{1/3}B^{2/3}$.</li>
</ol>
</details>

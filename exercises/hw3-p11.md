Show that
\[ F(z)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-t^2+zt}\,dt \]
defines an entire function $F$. Then find $F$ in closed form by first taking $z$ real. You may use $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-t^2}\,dt=\sqrt\pi$.
<details class="sol"><summary>Hint</summary>
On each disk $|z|\lt R$, differentiate under the integral sign ([A.4](apx:A4)). For real $z$, complete the square. The two entire functions agree on $\mathbb R$, so @thm:identity-theorem-l10 extends the formula.
</details>
<details class="sol"><summary>Solution</summary>
<ol>
<li><em>Entire.</em> Fix $R\gt0$ and let $|z|\lt R$. The integrand $h(z,t)=e^{-t^2+zt}$ is entire in $z$ for each $t$, and $|h|=e^{-t^2+t\operatorname{Re} z}\le e^{-t^2+R|t|}$. Also $|\partial_z h|=|t|\,|h|\le|t|e^{-t^2+R|t|}$. Both dominating functions are integrable on $\mathbb R$. By the complex-parameter form of [differentiation under the integral](apx:A4), $F$ is holomorphic on $|z|\lt R$. Every point of $\mathbb C$ lies in such a disk, so $F$ is entire.</li>
<li><em>Real $z$.</em> $-t^2+zt=-(t-z/2)^2+z^2/4$. For real $z$ the change of variables $u=t-z/2$ gives
\[ F(z)=e^{z^2/4}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-u^2}\,du=\sqrt\pi\,e^{z^2/4}. \]</li>
<li><em>All $z$.</em> The function $\sqrt\pi\,e^{z^2/4}$ is entire ([A.19](apx:A19)). It agrees with $F$ on $\mathbb R$. The sequence $1/n$ lies in $\mathbb R$, avoids $0$, and converges to $0\in\mathbb C$. By @thm:identity-theorem-l10 the two entire functions agree on $\mathbb C$. So $F(z)=\sqrt\pi\,e^{z^2/4}$.</li>
</ol>
</details>
